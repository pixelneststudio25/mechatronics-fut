Accessibility

How we are making this website usable for everyone, and where it still falls short.

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Our commitment

We want everyone to be able to use this website, including people who use screen readers, keyboards, voice control or zoom, and people on slow connections or older phones. We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA.

This site has not yet had an independent accessibility audit, so we cannot say it fully meets that standard. This page explains what we have done and where we know there are gaps.

What we have done

  • Every page can be used with a keyboard alone, with a clear focus outline.
  • A "Skip to main content" link appears first on every page.
  • Pages use proper headings and landmarks so screen readers can move around easily.
  • The full-screen menu can be closed with the Escape key, keeps keyboard focus inside while open, and tells screen readers whether it is open.
  • Images that carry meaning have text descriptions. Purely decorative graphics are hidden from screen readers.
  • Text and background colours were chosen for strong contrast on the dark theme.
  • Animations stop or slow down for visitors who have asked their device to reduce motion.
  • Text can be enlarged and pages reflow to fit small screens without side-scrolling.
  • Form fields have visible labels, and error messages are announced and shown next to the field.
  • Pages are light, with self-hosted fonts and compressed images, to help on slow connections.

Known limitations

  • Uploaded documents. Notes, past questions and other files added to the Resources page, including files kept on Google Drive, may not be fully accessible, for example scanned pages without searchable text.
  • Photos. Some gallery photos have only short descriptions.
  • No formal audit. Some issues may not have been found yet.

Tell us about a problem
